## M3 — The player's body (`m3-player`, 2026-11-03)

Sam: the fingers looked bad even in a fist, holding a tool "deletes the arms and glues fake ones on", the walk looked stiff; wanted Lethal Company's body — your own arms actually reaching and gripping, procedural legs — on a sturdier lumberjack. Asset Store options were offered for comparison; this is the in-house version on the current model.

- **Real fingers.** Meshy's rig had none. `FingerRig.Graft` adds three joints per finger under each hand, placed from Sam's Mixamo rig of the same mesh, and re-skins the body with Mixamo's finger weights (four influences, the rest scaled down). Each joint bends towards the palm about its own +x (the thumb across it). The joints' bind poses go through the hand bone's skin matrix, because Meshy's rig is not in its bind pose (the first try exploded the arms into sheets); `PlayerRigTests` checks the re-skinned body at rest matches the model to 2 mm. The baked fist/grip meshes are gone.
- **Hand poses at run time.** `HandPose` eases each hand to a `HandShape`: relaxed, fist (punching), grip (the axe, both hands), pistol (index crooked on the nail gun's trigger), can (wrapped round, index on the button). Each held item says its shape (`IHeldItem.Shape`).
- **The body's own arms.** `HeldArm` and its two camera-pinned arm copies are deleted. `ArmReach` puts the body's real right (and left, for the axe) arm on the tool's grip by two-bone IK, elbow out and down from the chest; on a handle the hollow of the curled fingers goes round it. First person, shadow and the body seen from outside are one skeleton.
- **Eyes in the head.** The camera was a fixed 1.7 m on the controller, about 15 cm above the model's eyes (24 cm above the head bone in the idle stance), so you looked down through the cut neck and the shoulders were too far below the view for the hands to reach a tool. Now `PlayerBody.PlaceEyes` mounts it on the animated neck at the measured eyes (now `EyeFromHead`, see below) and nods the head through half the look (`HeadNod`), so looking down the eyes come forward over the collar and you see your chest, belt and jeans; it follows crouching and the legs' hip drop, its height smoothed against the walk's bob. The body is slid so the neck is over the player's middle. A disabled controller (tests, cutscenes) leaves the camera alone.
- **Tools carried in front of the chest.** `ToolViewModel` places each tool in a frame that follows 65 % of the look up or down, so looking up or down it stays out in front where the arms reach it rather than in your face or belly; the gun still aims at the crosshair. Within 0.65 m of a wall the tool draws back and down towards you.
- **Legs.** `Legs` (after the animation): each foot ray-casts to the ground and goes there with the sole tilted to the slope (up to 30°), the hips dropping as far as the lower foot needs (up to 35 cm); standing still the feet plant where they are and a foot steps (0.24 s, 7 cm arc) when left 22 cm behind or turned 30° — so turning on the spot shuffles the feet. Measured in-frame on a slope: both feet exactly at ankle height over the ground.
- **Tools ride the stride; eyes higher (playtest).** Sam: walking and running, the arms moved but the axe and nail gun stayed put, so the wrists twisted; and looking down he saw his belt, not his shirt ("like a dwarf"). `PlayerBody.GaitOffset`/`GaitTurn`: the quick part of the chest's movement (the animated chest before the look leans it, in the body's own yaw frame, less a 0.4 s average) — the tool in hand rides with it, bobbing ~6 cm a stride. `ArmReach.ForearmShare` 0.75: the forearm turns through three quarters of the hand's twist instead of all of it at the wrist. Wrist twist running with the axe: 56° → 35°. Eyes: set 5 cm above the model's own, and the head now nods about the top of the neck (the head bone, `EyeFromHead`), not its base: about the base, looking down dropped the eyes below the collar and out past the chest. The chest leans only 10° with a look down (was 30°: a hunch curls the chest under you). Looking down you now see shirt, belt and jeans; no neck hole.
- **Chops land where you look (playtest).** Sam: the swing didn't hit where the crosshair was. `AxeTool` now knows the bite point (the trunk's face towards you at the chopping line) and `AxePose.AimFrom` turns the chop's strike so the blade's edge comes down on it, the hands staying near the strike's own place (within 0.6 m of the shoulders) and the axe swinging round them. For a low cut you bend at the waist (`PlayerBody.ChopBend`, up to 28°, in over the wind-up and out over the recovery; the camera goes with the head). Measured in game at the strike: the edge 6–28 cm from the bite and on the crosshair, standing 0.9–1.4 m from the trunk. A step-in towards the trunk was tried and removed at Sam's word: a chop never moves you.
- **A chill idle.** Sam: the idle turned left and right too much. Meshy's idle stands relaxed, then looks right round to the left and the right (face through ~240°, shoulders ~155°). `CharacterGenerator.StillestTime` finds its stillest face-forward moment (the relaxed stance it starts and ends in: face 0°, shoulders 16°) and `HoldStill` holds it; `PlayerBody.Breathe` adds a slow breath standing still (1.2° chest rise every 4.5 s, the head kept level), fading out as you move. Test: `PlayerRigTests.Idle_StandsStill_FacingTheFront`. Coplay's Meshy animation-library search returned "function not found" this session, so a different clip couldn't be fetched.
- **Strides.** `CharacterGenerator.StrideSpeed` measures each clip's real pace from its planted foot: walk 1.41, run 3.55, back-walk 1.26, crouch walk 1.24 m/s. The blend places each clip at its pace, stretched up to 1.5× before the next takes over, and `MoveScale` speeds the whole state beyond the fastest, so the feet keep pace with the ground. Sam then slowed walking 1.5× (4 → 2.67 m/s) and sprinting 1.7× (7 → 4.12 m/s), then sprint 1.2× faster (4.94 m/s); measured in game, the animator's speed matches and the feet keep pace.
- **Tests:** `PlayerRigTests` (hand shapes, finger bending, leg step arc / hip drop / strain, move scale, arm reach on grip and handle, weight merging, real finger bones skinned and matching the model at rest, stride speeds, eye geometry against the model's measurements). `CharacterTests` updated. `Tools/Cabin/Build/Generate Player` rebuilds just the player.
- **Not done:** the model itself is unchanged (the Meshy lumberjack). Everything above is on Unity's bones by name, so a better model with the same humanoid naming swaps in; its eye and neck numbers would need measuring again (`EyeFromNeck`). Not benched (two ray casts and a few IK solves a frame).

## M5b — Free build (replaces M5a; see `docs/handoffs/Handoff_CabinGame_FreeBuild_v2.md`)

**Why:** Sam played M5a (floor/wall/roof layout tools on a flat platform) and rejected it: generators hold your hand.
Handoff v2 asks for real pieces you carry, freeze, nail and saw. M5a's code is gone from the tree (git history only).

**How to play:** `Tools/Cabin/Scenes/Open TestPlatform`, press Play, click the Game view. Maximise the Game view (Shift+Space) — the HUD is sized for 1080p and up.

**What exists**
- **Scene:** a 240 m rolling terrain with a level clearing (generated, seeded, `Assets/Generated/TestTerrain/`), ~400 placeholder spruce, two rows of lumber racks (2×4 and 4×4 in 8/10/12/14/16/20 ft), plywood racks (4×8, 4×10, 4×12), a cutting table, the Player and BuildSystem. `Tools/Cabin/Scenes/Rebuild TestPlatform` regenerates all of it; `Tools/Cabin/Build/Generate Sandbox Assets` regenerates textures (procedural wood/plywood/grass/dirt), materials, the nail head, tree, Player and BuildSystem prefabs.
- **Scale:** `Stock.CrossSectionScale = 3` (a 2×4 shows as 10.5 × 4.5 in) and `Stock.LengthScale = 1.25` (an "8 ft" board is 10 ft of world; racks and the HUD show the nominal length via `Stock.Nominal`). Playtest 3: "8 ft boards should be 2 ft taller". Two constants.
- **Pieces** (`Cabin.Build.Piece`): a `ConvexSolid` (vertices + face loops) in the stock's frame, flat-shaded mesh, exact convex `MeshCollider`. Any plane cut gives two convex solids (1/8 in kerf), so colliders stay exact after any number of cuts.
- **Structures** (`Structure`): nailed-together pieces share one Rigidbody (compound collider, mass from wood volume). Kinematic while any member is planted / frozen / clamped / carried; otherwise dynamic and it falls. Regrouped from the nail graph after every nail, pull or cut; split halves inherit velocity.
- **Posts and deck blocks (the foundation):** there is **no planting in bare ground** (playtest 3: the vertical snap felt uncontrollable). A 4×4 is an ordinary free-form piece — it comes off the rack flat like a 2×4 and freezes like one. The one snap: a 4×4 aimed at an empty, planted **deck block** previews seated 6 in into the socket, plumb and centred, whatever way it was held; LMB seats it (`Planted`, `SeatedIn` = block). **Deck blocks** (`StockKind.DeckBlock`, concrete frustum, can't be sawn or nailed) aimed at the ground preview level (yaw kept) with the base below the lowest ground under all four corners plus a sink, so no corner floats; LMB sets them. Planted = kinematic: nothing knocks a block or a seated post over. A block with a post in it can't be lifted; a seated post can (it unseats).
- **Clamps (playtest 5, replaces freeze):** LMB with the held piece pressed against other wood clamps it there: `ContactFinder` finds every piece it touches (alternating closest points on the convex colliders; flush contacts resolved by a separating-face test), `ClampLayout` puts one clamp per touched piece (nearest 3) or two along a single long overlap, each `ClampVisual` an orange C-clamp sized to the combined thickness, hooked over the held piece's nearest edge, growing in/shrinking out. Not touching anything → it stays in your hands and the HUD says "press it against another piece". One clamped piece at a time: clamping another releases the first (it falls unless nailed). E on a clamp takes them off. Clamps re-fit after a nail pull or a cut, and are saved (re-clamped on load). Clamp colliders are triggers on their own layer and never touch physics.
- **Player character (playtest after PS2 attempt: "two sets of shoulders… grey haired… short brown hair with light beard, cartoonish realism PS2"):** concept image (`Art/Models/Reference/Lumberjack_v3_Ref.png`: early-30s lumberjack, short brown hair, light stubble, plaid, suspenders, jeans, boots) → Meshy 6 image-to-3D (`Lumberjack_v3.glb`, 12k tris) → **auto-rigged by Meshy** (`Lumberjack_v3_Rigged.glb` + walking/running) → Meshy clips Idle 0, Regular_Jump 466, Heavy_Hammer_Swing 128, Cautious_Crouch_Walk_Forward_inplace 616. The earlier PS2 body was a low-poly mesh skinned onto the old rig by weight transfer (Meshy's rigging was failing then); its shoulders didn't match the old joints, hence the doubled shoulder — that approach and its assets are gone. `CharacterMeshes` builds `Lumberjack.prefab`: the PS2 look (texture 512² cut to 16-bit colour, no shine) and three renderers on one skeleton — `Body_Full` (shadow in first person, everything in pilot/fly), `Body_FirstPersonTorso` (everything inside Sam's hand-placed red boxes in `Art/Models/Lumberjack_HeadCut.prefab` is cut away — `Tools/Cabin/Build/Edit First-Person Head Cut` opens it; boxes are any child named HeadCut*, measured against the reference lumberjack; triangles crossing a box face are sliced, not dropped. No right arm) and `Body_FirstPersonRightArm` (split by skin weights). `CharacterGenerator` (`Tools/Cabin/Build/Generate Player Character`, also run by Generate Sandbox Assets) makes the clips and `Lumberjack.controller`: Locomotion (idle 0 / walk 2.5 / run 6.5 m/s on Speed), Crouch (one-frame crouch idle / crouch walk at 2 m/s) on the Crouch bool, Jump trigger (cut to take-off → landing, hips held to standing so the clip doesn't add height), Hammer trigger on an upper-body layer (mask from the spine up). Regenerating writes over asset files in place so GUIDs survive. Clip IDs come from Meshy's public catalogue (`api.meshy.ai/web/public/animations/resources`) because Coplay's `search_animation_library` is broken.
- **Punch (Hands, nothing held):** hold LMB to draw the right fist back (power fills over 0.8 s, shown as a bar), let go to throw it — a straight, jab or hook at random, never the same twice (Meshy 210 Boxing_Guard_Prep_Straight_Punch, 192 Right_Jab_from_Guard, 195 Right_Upper_Hook_from_Guard; `CharacterGenerator.Punches` lists the cut points), each cut into a wind-up that holds and a strike played 1.1–1.8× by power; the power bar only appears after holding 0.5 s, on the upper-body layer at full weight. Lands when that punch's fist is fully out: a 1.3 m ray from the eye shoves loose wood (6–70 N·s); fixed pieces just say Thud. The clip stands side-on, so its hip turn is folded into the spine; Meshy's idle also stood with the hips turned up to 60°, now straightened the same way (legs face forward when standing). Meshy hands have no finger bones, so the fist is an open hand.
- **Clamps rebuilt (playtest: stretched, clipping):** `ContactFinder` returns the exact touching patch (the two faces' overlap polygon, any angle). `ClampLayout` tries every edge direction in the contact plane (the pieces' axes and the diagonals between them), slides the clamp to that edge of the patch, measures the jaws as the pieces' real thickness at that spot and the reach as how far the wood actually goes round it there, rejects any frame that would overlap other wood or the ground, and keeps the shortest. Reach is capped at a real clamp's throat (0.35 m); a contact with no sensible spot gets no clamp rather than a stretched one. Long overlaps get two clamps near the ends, wrapping across. `ClampLayoutTests` (5).
- **Nail targeting:** looking near a nail counts — the nearest head within ~3° or 5 cm of the crosshair, not behind the wood you see, is outlined and X pulls it.
- **Tool sounds:** nail gun thunk-and-hiss per shot (6 clips) and a few saw strokes per cut (3), cut from generated recordings.
- **HUD (Sam picked mockup A · Clean):** `BuildHud` — dot crosshair; 8-slot hotbar at the bottom (1 Nail gun, 2 Saw, 3 Spray paint, 4–8 empty = Hands; start on 4) with icons from `IconGenerator` (models rendered to Art/UI; saw icon drawn, Art/UI/Source/Icon_Saw.svg) and the spray colour as a dot on its slot; item name fades in above on a switch; one short prompt under the crosshair only when there's something to do, keys as caps ("[E] Pick up"), optional dim second line; message pill; thin punch bar. F1 controls, F3 fps/counts. Tool hints, counters, the controls line and the long status lines are gone.
- **Fists and grips:** the rig has no finger bones, so `CharacterMeshes.Curl` bends the mesh: each finger folds at its three joints (13 / 16.4 / 19.2 cm up the hand bone) about hinges on the palm side, blended over the joints; the thumb turns in. `_Fist` (balled, swapped in while punching) and `_Grip` (round a handle: the axe in both hands). Camera FOV 72 (was 62); the hands' overlay camera stays 62; tools keep the open hand. Punch whoosh on the throw, knock on a hit (`Audio/Punch_*`, hits cut from `Source_Punch_Wood.wav`). Punch bar after 0.25 s.
- **Player sounds:** `PlayerSounds` on the Player — a footstep per stride (1.3 m at the walk clip's 2.5 m/s to 2 m at the run's 6.5 m/s — what the legs cover per step; quieter crouched), grass or wood by what's underfoot (a Piece is wood; anything else grass); a push-off on jumping; a landing thud after any fall of ≥0.2 s at ≥3 m/s (jumped or walked off an edge), louder the harder. Random clip, never the same twice running, ±7% pitch. Sounds made with Coplay's SFX generator: single-step prompts came out near-silent, so walking recordings (`Audio/Source_Walk_*.wav`) were sliced into steps and levelled (7 grass, 6 wood); jump ×2, landing ×2 per surface levelled the same way.
- **Spray paint (playtest: slow, short):** range 2.5 → 4.5 m, paint per droplet 0.18 → 0.4 with the fall-off stretched over 2.6 m, 3,500/8,000 droplets/s (was 2,500/5,000); runs need more paint to start (2.5 → 4).
- **Directional legs (playtest):** Speed is signed along the way the legs face (back-walk −2.5/−6.5 m/s from Meshy Walk_Backward_inplace 679, crouch walk reversed for backing up). `PlayerBody` turns the hips towards the actual velocity (up to 90° either side; the back-walk turned the same way past ~110°, with hysteresis) and the spine back by the same amount, so strafing and diagonals — and any controller stick angle — step that way while the chest faces where you look. Rotated after the animation, eased at 10/s.
- **Full-body first person:** you see your own body. `PlayerBody` draws the headless torso (chest, belt, legs, boots, left arm) and casts the full body's shadow. After the animation each frame it slides the body horizontally so the head sits 10 cm behind the camera (the camera at the eyes) — whatever the pose (idle sway, crouch lean, a swing) — and up to 20 cm further back as you look down, so looking down shows your front, not your collarbones. Crouch eye height raised to match the crouch pose (capsule 1.1 → 1.4 m). With a tool out, `ToolViewModel` (an `IHeldItem`) gives a grip and a `HeldArm` holds it: a copy of the lumberjack's right arm fixed to the view-model camera, lined up with the body at a level look, so the reach is the same at every look angle (the body's arm hung from an unpitched shoulder, so looking far up or down contorted it); the body's own right arm is hidden meanwhile. Each frame from rest: `TwoBoneIK` to the grip (elbow out-and-down) and the hand set to the grip's turn — the plain hand Sam preferred to the fist/forearm-roll attempt. The nail gun turns its nose to whatever the crosshair is on (eased; at least 1.2 m out, 25 m with nothing there), so it points where the nail goes and the arm bends to follow. The spray now leaves from the can's actual nozzle. The hammer swing plays at 40 % in first person (full in pilot view) so your aim doesn't jump.
- **Nail gun fires anywhere (playtest):** LMB always fires. Aimed through one piece into another it drives a joining nail as before; anywhere else it fires a loose nail (`NailShot`, 30 m/s with a short streak) that sticks where it hits — wood (rides with the piece, follows cuts and re-centres), ground, trees — or flies off past 40 m. Loose nails are visual only, not saved, capped at 300, cleared with F8. Joining nails also fly in; their head appears as the shot lands.
- **Tool models in hand:** a framing nail gun (piston head with exhaust cap and band, tapered drive housing, steel nose and safety yoke, depth wheel, rubber-gripped pistol handle, trigger, brass air fitting, aluminium stick magazine with a nail strip) and an aerosol can (turned steel body with rolled rims and concave base, domed top, valve cup, actuator with nozzle, label band) built in code by `ToolModelGenerator` (`Tools/Cabin/Build/Generate Tool Models`, also run by Generate Sandbox Assets) from `ModelKit` chamfered/tapered convex blocks and lathed profiles, real size. Shown by `ToolViewModel` on an overlay camera (layer `ViewModel`, stacked on the player camera) so they never clip into walls: nail gun for tool 2, can for tool 4; sway behind the mouse, recoil kick per nail, the can's button presses while spraying and its label takes the paint colour (own material — a MaterialPropertyBlock rendered garbage on the overlay camera with the GPU Resident Drawer). Hidden while carrying or piloting. The spray now leaves from the can's nozzle.
- **Spray paint (4):** hold LMB to spray where the crosshair is; the can sits in your right hand. Droplets (2,500/s fine cap, 5,000/s wide) leave the nozzle in a gaussian cone (σ 1.6° fine, 5° wide) and each one is a raycast, so only reachable surfaces get paint: things in front shadow what's behind, back faces and grazing angles get little. Close up the spot is tight and heavy (lettering); far away it's wide and light; past 2.5 m the paint has dried to dust. Paint sprayed onto already-covered paint on a steep face builds "wetness" and runs into drips that slow and stop. Scroll cycles a 12-colour palette, R swaps fine/wide cap, C opens a colour picker (palette + hue/saturation/brightness). Visible mist particles and a procedural hiss. Under the hood each piece gets a `PaintAtlas` (every face unfolded at true scale, 4 mm texels, shelf-packed, max 2048²) as UV1, and on first paint a `PaintLayer`: an RGBA texture shown by a transparent URP Lit overlay lifted 0.6 mm off the faces. Paint follows cuts and re-centring (texels re-projected into the new layout; fresh cut faces are bare wood) and is saved as a PNG per piece. Works on wood and concrete.
- **Lining up deck blocks (playtest 7):** while placing a block, a dashed line runs to each set block within 8 m (nearest 4): green when the new block sits on one of that block's square directions, yellow otherwise, labelled with degrees off and the centre-to-centre distance as a nominal length. Within 3° the new block snaps onto the nearest block's line and takes its rotation; Shift places freely. A 4×4 seated in a block always takes the nearest of the block's four square orientations, so it can't be twisted in the socket.
- **Handling (playtest 6):** the held piece's rotation is stored relative to the player's facing, so turning round keeps the same face towards you (world rotation while piloting, so orbiting doesn't turn it). **R** turn about vertical, **T** tip about your right, **Y** roll about your forward — 90°, Shift for 15°; **V** squares to the world; RMB-drag locks to the first direction you drag (horizontal = turn, vertical = tip), Shift snaps it to 15°. New stock spawns at a clear spot in front of you (`CarryController.TakeNew` searches for a pose that overlaps nothing), and the hands back off from ground/racks/benches by the piece's support distance along the surface normal, so nothing starts or gets wedged in the pile; wood can still be pressed against. After every cut both halves are **re-centred** (origin at the middle of the shape, nail records and heads corrected) so an offcut handles from its own middle.
- **Pilot mode (G, while holding):** the camera leaves the body and orbits the held piece (mouse orbits, scroll zooms, kept above ground); WASD moves the piece in the camera's horizontal frame, Space/Ctrl up/down, Shift slows to 0.5 m/s for fine work, RMB + mouse spins it, R/F still work. LMB places / E drops / G returns; the camera snaps back onto the body (`FirstPersonController.ExternalControl`). The piece is still physical while piloted.
- **Line-up helpers:** **F** aligns the held piece to the piece under the crosshair (grain to grain, face to face); **Shift+F** turns it across (a board lands on edge across a post's face). The HUD shows a live level/plumb readout for the held piece ("level ✓ · roll 1.2°").
- **Hands (1):** **E** takes stock from a rack / picks up a loose, frozen or clamped piece (nailed pieces refuse: pull nails first) and **E again drops it**. **LMB places**: clamp it to what it's pressed against, seat a 4×4 in a block, set a block, lay a piece on the cutting table. The held piece is a real physics body (layer `HeldPiece`, ignores the player and the aim ray, weightless, 3 kg) steered by velocity towards the hands, so it presses against posts and the ground instead of passing through; freezing pins it exactly where physics left it. Snapped previews (post into the ground, flat on the table) are posed kinematically instead. The hands float where you look; scroll = distance (Shift: fine), hold RMB + mouse = rotate it (camera look is suppressed), R = square up to world axes (Shift+R: turn 90°), LMB = drop. **E** = freeze in place; a vertical 4×4 aimed at the ground snaps to stand 18 in deep and E **plants** it (anchor); aimed at the cutting table E **clamps** it flat on the top. X on a nail head pulls it.
- **Nail gun (2):** LMB drives a 3.5 in (scaled) nail through the piece you're looking at into the one behind it. Rules (`NailRules`): must pass through the first piece, ≥ 1/2 in bite. Gaps ≤ 1/4 in are nailed as they are; gaps up to **2 in are pulled flush** first: the unanchored structure (the piece being nailed if free, else the one behind) slides along the nail line if the end pose is clear (try-move + penetration check); if that's blocked and it's the clamped piece, it **swings round its farthest clamp** until it touches (bisected so it never jams). ## M1 — Map (Nova Scotia)

**How to play:** `Tools/Cabin/Scenes/Open World`, Play. You start in a clearing by a lake near Kejimkujik.

**Redesigned 2026-10-31** (spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-terrain-water-redesign-design.md`). Sam's playtest of the first version: lakes and rivers square and jagged, terrain flat; the "dramatic" version had broken waterfalls. Root cause: the map shrinks Nova Scotia 68×, so OpenStreetMap's real lakes were 20–80 m ponds stamped onto noise terrain that never agreed with them. Now the terrain and the water come from one simulation; nothing is drawn from an outline.

- `MapRecipe` (`Assets/_Project/Data/MapRecipe.asset`) drives `Tools/Cabin/Map/Generate World` (~55 s). **Changing a recipe default: delete the recipe asset first** (the asset keeps its old values; `Recipe()` recreates it).
- **Coarse geology, 8 m grid** (`coarseCell`): real elevation (AWS Terrain Tiles, cached in `Library/CabinElevation`) gives only the coastline and the broad highs and lows; `ProvinceMask` sinks everything that isn't Nova Scotia. `BaseTerrain` lays relief on by region — rolling lowland, ridged and terraced highland (Cape Breton, Cobequids, North/South Mountain; `regionHigh` 250 m real), sea cliffs on rugged coast — and a **hardness** field: soft lowland, and a hard band capping every highland terrace step. `Erosion` runs 80 iterations of implicit stream-power incision (Braun & Willett 2013) with hardness-weighted diffusion and a per-cell incision cap (15 m lowland, 70 m highland): rivers cut their own valleys; hard bands hold as knickpoints. `Basins` then scoops glacial lake basins (two blob sizes, dug below the local lowest ground so each holds one sheet of water) on gentle lowland, densest in the lake country round the spawn.
- **Water from drainage** (`Drainage`, `WaterNetwork`): priority-flood fill; every basin fills to its spill and that is a lake at that level (hollows under 20 cells are filled in); cells draining ≥ 0.03 game km² are rivers, traced from each mouth upstream along the biggest flow, levels from the filled surface (never rising downstream), widths 3–18 m from the catchment, courses smoothed (Chaikin); a drop steeper than 0.35 totalling ≥ 2.5 m is a `Fall`. Data: `Lake`, `River`, `Fall`.
- **Fine ground, 2 m** (`FineTerrain`): bicubic upsample, detail noise damped within ~2 m of any water level, a gentle shelf either side of each lake's waterline (beach above, shallows below, clean waterline), lake beds deepened from the shore, river channels carved along the curves (rounded bed, short bank, bank never below the water); at a fall the bed drops to the base level at the lip (a cliff face) with a plunge pool.
- **Meshes and shader:** lakes as before (per-tile quads extended under the shore); rivers as ribbons per tile (`WaterMeshes.Rivers`) continuing over each fall as a near-vertical sheet flagged in UV2.y; `Cabin/Water` has a falling mode (streaks scrolling down, foam, opaque). Sea unchanged.

## M4 (part) — Axe

**How to play:** World scene, slot **4** (Axe). Walk up to a tree and look at the lower trunk.

- Within 2.7 m and looking at the bottom ~2 m of a trunk, the prompt says Chop (the ring guide was removed: Sam found it distracting). **LMB** swings sideways into the line; anywhere else it's an overhead swing that knocks loose wood about.
- The first chop turns the terrain tree into a `ChoppingTree` carrying the tree's own mesh (`NotchedMesh`): the trunk round the chopping line is split into rows and sectors (branches untouched), so until it's cut it looks exactly as it stood; the notch moves those vertices in and the cut faces become flat-shaded fresh wood. On the fall the mesh is cut through at the line, stump and butt each get a wood face. Each chop takes the notch's flat face 4.5 cm deeper (±15 %), square to where you stand, so it widens as it deepens; fresh wood shows in the cut, chips fly out, chop sound. Chopping from another side carries on from wherever the notch already reaches that way.
- When less than 32 % of the cross-section is left, the top goes over **away from you** on a hinge at the back of the trunk, creaks, lets go at 55° and crashes down (capsule colliders for trunk and crown; the crown's shrinks until it clears neighbouring trees, else it would start inside them and be thrown). Thin birch ≈ 3 chops, a typical spruce ≈ 5, thick trunks 8+.
- The terrain data is copied on the first chop, so chopping never changes the generated assets; **felled trees come back next run** (no persistence yet).
- Axe model: 28 in felling axe (oval S-curved hickory handle with a hooked knob, red head lofted poll to edge, bright ground bevel).
- **Not done:** two-handed grip and swing (one hand holds it for now — next up), bucking logs, stump removal, carrying logs, the axe as a weapon against anything but loose wood.
